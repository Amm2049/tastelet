TASTELET: PRESENTATION SCRIPT (5 MINUTES INCLUDING DEMO)

Talk about 2:40, demo about 1:15, buffer about 1:00.
Before the talk: open https://tastelet.vercel.app 5 minutes early so the free backend is awake,
and keep the tab ready next to the slides.

1. TITLE (0:10)

Hi, this is Tastelet. You pick five movies you love, and a neural network gives you
a taste persona and twenty recommendations.

2. THE COLD-START PROBLEM (0:25)

The model learned from two hundred thousand MovieLens users. But a visitor has no
account and no ratings, so the model knows nothing about them. That's the cold-start problem.
So we ask for five to ten favourite movies instead.

3. WHY NCF (0:25)

Popularity gives everyone the same list. Content-based only sees genres.
Neural collaborative filtering learns real taste, and adds a new user without retraining.
The trade-off: a movie with no ratings can't be recommended.

4. DATA AND TRAINING (0:20)

We trained on MovieLens: 32 million ratings. Each user's older ratings train the model,
the newer ones test it, so it never sees the future.

5. FROM PICKS TO PERSONA (0:30)

When you press the button: we freeze the model and learn a vector just for you,
starting from people who love your picks. Then we rank all movies,
and your nearest of seven clusters gives your persona.

6. HIT@10 HID PROBLEMS (0:35)

Hit@10 asks: is a movie the user really liked in our top ten?
We beat popularity, and give far more personal lists: 997 different movies against 132.
But the score hid two problems: the plain model lost to popularity, and a Marvel fan got Fight Club.
So: one metric isn't enough. Look at real lists.

LIKELY QUESTIONS (not part of the 5 minutes)

- Why not just use popularity, if it's that close? Popularity gives all 1,000 users nearly the same
  list (132 different movies). NCF matches it on Hit@10 and gives each user their own list (997).
- Why not item-KNN? On the small v1 data item-KNN had a better Hit@10 (0.54 vs 0.47), but it gives no
  user vectors, so no fold-in for new users and no personas.
- What is Hit@10? For each test user: is at least one movie they really liked next in our top 10?
  The score is the share of users where it is (0.224 = 22.4% of users).
- What is fold-in? Keep all trained weights fixed and train only one new user vector (100 steps)
  so the picks get high scores. It takes about a second, no retraining.
- Why a time split? It copies real use: predict what someone likes next from what they liked before.
  A random split lets the model see "future" ratings and makes results look better than they are.
- Why 7 personas? K = 7 was the only value from 6 to 10 where no two clusters shared 2 of their top 3
  genres (no look-alike personas).
- Why can't it recommend new movies? NCF learns a vector for a movie from its ratings. A movie with
  no ratings has no vector (item cold start).
- Is my data stored? No. Picks are never stored or logged.
