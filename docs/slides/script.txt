TASTELET: PRESENTATION SCRIPT (5 MINUTES)

About 130 words per minute. Times are a guide; the total is about 5:00.
Before the talk: open https://tastelet.vercel.app 5 minutes early so the free backend is awake.

1. TITLE (0:20)

Hi, this is Tastelet, a movie recommender. You pick five to ten movies you love, and a neural network
gives you a taste persona and twenty recommendations. The interesting part: it works for someone the
model has never seen before.

2. THE COLD-START PROBLEM (0:40)

The model learned taste from about two hundred thousand MovieLens users and their past ratings.
But a visitor to our app is not one of them. There are no accounts and no past ratings, so the model
knows nothing about them. That's the cold-start problem.
So instead, we ask for five to ten favourite movies, and return a persona, a genre sentence and
twenty matches. Here you see the picker, and the results page.

3. WHY NCF (0:45)

We compared three approaches. Popularity is simple and surprisingly strong, but everyone gets the
same list, so it's our baseline. Content-based models can handle new movies, but they only see
genres, not taste.
We chose neural collaborative filtering. It learns taste from thirty-two million ratings, and a new
user can be added without retraining. Its user vectors also give us the personas.
The trade-off: it can't recommend a movie that has no ratings yet.

4. DATA AND TRAINING (0:40)

We trained on MovieLens 32M: twenty-three thousand movies and two hundred thousand users, with
ratings up to 2023.
We split by time for each user: their oldest ratings train the model, their newest ones test it,
so the model never peeks at the future.
The model gives every user and every movie a vector of 32 numbers, and a small neural network turns
a pair into a score. We measure Hit@10 against the popularity baseline.

5. FROM PICKS TO PERSONA (0:45)

Here's what happens when you press "See my results".
One, fold-in: we freeze the trained model and learn only a vector for you, so your picks score high.
Two, we start that search from the people who love your picks, not from the average user.
Three, we rank all movies, with a small popularity blend and a boost for newer movies.
Four, your vector's nearest of seven K-means clusters gives your persona.
Same picks always give the same result, in about one second.

6. HIT@10 HID PROBLEMS (0:55)

On a thousand test users with five picks, NCF scores 0.224 Hit@10, against 0.211 for popularity.
And the lists are far more personal: 997 different movies across all users, against 132.
But Hit@10 hid two problems. First, the plain model actually lost to popularity, at 0.159. A small
popularity blend fixed that.
Second, a Marvel fan got Fight Club. Hit@10 didn't show this at all; we only caught it by looking at
real lists. Starting from the picks' fans fixed it.
The lesson: check variety and real lists, not just one metric.

7. TRY IT (0:15)

You can try it now: scan the code or open tastelet.vercel.app.
Next, a content-based model could recommend brand-new releases. Thank you. Any questions?

------------------------------------------------------------

LIKELY QUESTIONS

- Why not just use popularity, if it's that close? Popularity gives all 1,000 users nearly the same
  list (132 different movies). NCF matches it on Hit@10 and gives each user their own list (997).
- Why not item-KNN? On the small v1 data item-KNN had a better Hit@10 (0.54 vs 0.47), but it gives no
  user vectors, so no fold-in for new users and no personas.
- What is Hit@10? For each test user: is at least one movie they really liked next in our top 10?
  The score is the share of users where it is.
- What is fold-in? Keep all trained weights fixed and train only one new user vector (100 steps)
  so the picks get high scores. It takes about a second, no retraining.
- Why a time split? It copies real use: predict what someone likes next from what they liked before.
  A random split lets the model see "future" ratings and makes results look better than they are.
- Why 7 personas? K = 7 was the only value from 6 to 10 where no two clusters shared 2 of their top 3 genres
  (no look-alike personas).
- Why can't it recommend new movies? NCF learns a vector for a movie from its ratings. A movie with
  no ratings has no vector (item cold start).
- Is my data stored? No. Picks are never stored or logged.
